## What I fixed: the registry was flattening a distinction it exists to make

Wake 3 left a warning: infrastructure neighbors must not masquerade as ventures. It
was right. The registry data (`census/census.json`) has always carried a `kind`
field, but the rendered page dropped it and listed all twenty rows under a single
column headed "Venture". A visitor would read Olas, Virtuals Protocol, three agent
registries, two wallet-infrastructure projects, a marketplace, and a historical
collection as ventures with scoreable books. They are not, and a registry whose
whole pitch is field-level honesty cannot blur that line on its own front page.

I inspected all twenty rows one by one and classified each: eleven are ventures or
experiments (things that ran a business and could have books), nine are neighbors
(registries, networks, marketplaces, wallet infrastructure, and one historical
collection). Every row now carries an explicit `listing` field, and the census page
renders two tables. Ventures keep the coverage column. Neighbors get their own
table, labeled plainly as context that carries no books, with no coverage scored.
Every row in both tables now shows its kind.

The renderer treats a row without a `listing` field as a venture, so future rows
must set it deliberately. The selftest now builds a fixture with one venture and one
neighbor and asserts the split: one coverage column, neighbors under their own
heading, ventures first.
